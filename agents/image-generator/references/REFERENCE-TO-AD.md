# Turn a reference into a selling argument

Applied correction: September 30, 2026. The [package audit](../../../resources/research/2026-09-30-native-reference-audit.md) found that our rejected hoodie jobs skipped actual layout inputs and substituted mood plus headline for a product argument. See the [visual study](../../../resources/copywriting/native-ads/SACRED-OAK-STUDY.md).

## Before generation

Resolve the **chosen avatar and angle** from the product's [copy brief](../../../templates/COPY-BRIEF.md). For a new audience, use [avatar-angle research](../../../workflow/AVATAR-ANGLE-RESEARCH.md) before treating an imagined motivation as evidence. Avatar, situation and objections determine the scene and argument; reference selection follows them.

Write five concrete lines in the job, using the product/copy brief:

1. **Situation:** who is trying to do what, in which moment? Source or explicitly labeled hypothesis.
2. **Desired change:** the activity, frustration or experience that would be different.
3. **Product bridge:** the exact feature that could make that difference; show its truth photo.
4. **Reason to believe:** a demonstration, specification, credible observation or genuine matched feedback. Unsupported benefit stays out of copy.
5. **Click answer:** the question the ad opens and the exact page section that answers it.

If the same argument survives replacing the product with unrelated items, make the product bridge more specific. A supported gifting or identity angle can qualify; a universal functional-problem requirement would be an overcorrection. Never invent specifications to force a generic demo product into a stronger argument.

## Pick and pass the actual visual

Choose one inspected PNG from [INDEX](INDEX.md) per initial concept. State why that structure fits. Record:

- Reference ID and local image path; persuasive function, not just aesthetic.
- Reading order and zones to preserve: headline, annotations, demonstration, product, supporting copy.
- Exact new text and which words live in the image versus feed caption/headline.
- Brand, authority, review, offer or claim elements to replace or omit.
- Exact product photo as truth; reference PNG as structure. Supply both to generation and record actual input paths. If an original structure is a better fit, explain why instead of claiming it followed an unused reference.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 03 | Dominant editorial idea → three short explanations → product | A supported distinction answering a buyer question |
| 31 | Tight real-detail photograph → small annotation → explanatory feed headline | A detail we can show and explain; no invented danger |
| 42 | Comparison question → central product → numbered feature pointers | Verified buyer criteria/features; no fabricated tests or authority |
| 12 | Central product → spatially connected callouts | Each callout maps to a supported feature and credible benefit |
| 39 | Social situation → product action → practical objection | Reproducible demonstration; pictured action alone does not prove its result |

Consult the audit for viewed counterexamples. Library claims and winner labels are not inherited evidence. Never reproduce ad-library interface chrome, advertiser identity or fake endorsements in our creative.

## Review the whole message

Show **reference → generated concept → matching page answer**. Evaluate the image with its accompanying copy. Text density, a logo or a painted CTA does not make an ad complete. Honor this owner's request for readable in-image selling copy in the current batch; some supplied references instead put their explanation in the feed headline.

- Can a viewer name the reason to care and the product's role?
- Does the image explain/show something beyond decoration?
- Does output preserve the chosen structure and exact product details?
- Do claims, offer and destination agree? Is content readable at intended size?
- Record factual QC and actual owner response separately. Rejection reopens creative; do not leave it review-pending.
