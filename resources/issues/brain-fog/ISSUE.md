# Issue: I can't focus, I keep forgetting things, I lose my keys and the word I need

*One file per issue: `resources/issues/brain-fog/ISSUE.md`. Method and gate: [workflow/CHECKS.md](../../../workflow/CHECKS.md), Step 1. Researched 2026-10-01 by buyer-researcher (job issue-brainfog-2026-10-01, 35-minute time box). Every quote has a link and a date; unknown stays unknown. Everyday-life level only: this is not research into treating any condition. Where people mention hormones, ADHD, medication or dementia it is noted as context for understanding them, never as an angle. Our copy never tells the reader they have a condition and never promises memory, focus or cognitive improvement.*

## 1. The issue in one line

People describe a head that won't hold on to things: they walk into a room and forget why, lose keys and phones every day, lose the word they need in a meeting, and by the afternoon can't settle to anything. In their words: "I have no idea what's going on with me but I've become incredibly forgetful of late. Lots of dropping the ball at work and just forgetting appointments and even after school stuff." ([Mumsnet, 9 Feb 2025](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/general_health/5270794-horrible-brain-fog), S1). Their label for it is "brain fog" (also "woolly headed" and "dropping the ball").

## 2. What it feels like

Source numbers (S#) refer to section 9.

- **Embarrassment and fear of looking unprofessional:** "I know what I want to say but I have a blank. It's not so bad when I'm writing as I'm not on the spot but I'm afraid that I'm starting to look unprofessional." ([Mumsnet, 4 Jun 2025](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/general_health/5348440-perimenopause-and-losing-words-and-memory-when-you-talk-for-a-living), S6). "I sound incompetent some days and can't present in meetings as I just forget things." (same thread, S12).
- **Fear it is something serious:** "I've had an awful foreboding I'm in early onset dementia." ([Gransnet, 1 Feb 2025](https://www.gransnet.com/forums/chat/1345035-Short-term-memory-absolutely-appalling), S40). "I have now convinced myself this is the beginning of dementia." ([Gransnet, 24 Feb 2025](https://www.gransnet.com/forums/chat/1345776-Frightening-memory-loss), S43). *Interpretation: this fear is a big part of the emotion, and it is exactly the area ads must stay out of.*
- **Exhaustion from the daily hunt:** "It's tiring and exhausting." ([Mumsnet, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/_chat/5505721-constantly-losing-keys-purse-and-essentials-with-adhd-how-do-you-cope), S17).
- **Panic in the moment:** "I immediately felt panicked and frightened" after blanking on a route she walks weekly ([Gransnet, 24 Feb 2025](https://www.gransnet.com/forums/chat/1345776-Frightening-memory-loss), S42).
- **Wanting a plain explanation and a fix:** "It's terrible but I almost want to be ill. Not horribly unwell but with an identifiable issue that I can fix with a week or two of tablets and then get back to normal." ([Mumsnet, 12 Feb 2025](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/general_health/5270794-horrible-brain-fog), S3).
- **Overloaded, can't switch off:** "I feel like I've lost the ability to just focus on one task or even just relax and not try and tick something off my to-do list at the same time." ([Mumsnet, 1 May 2026](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/parenting/5524292-starting-to-worry-about-how-forgetful-i-am-since-having-2nd-child), S30).
- **Used to be sharp, now isn't (loss of identity):** "I was the high school valedictorian, I used to be able to understand things fast and pick up and truly zone on things I was working on." ([Blind, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.teamblind.com/post/foggy-brain-cant-retain-information-well-uf5pajug), S48). "I had great memory, could focus well." ([Blind, 9 Aug 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/cognitive-declining-0f8hjchp), S49).
- **Humour and shared relief among older adults:** "Very familiar daily occurrences" and the thread mostly answers "Same here" (Gransnet, 1 Feb 2025, S36 to S39). *Interpretation: this group normalises it with jokes, which is a different tone from the anxious 40s.*
- **Relief when a tool works:** "If you've ever lost anything important, like your keys, this is a Must Have!" ([Walmart review, 21 Oct 2024](https://www.walmart.com/reviews/product/660096564), S50).

### The moment and its cost (I2, checked 2026-10-03)

**The moment that keeps coming back:** the lap of the house before every trip out, hunting for keys and bags that were put somewhere "safe"; and the blank in the middle of a meeting. It repeats daily.
**What it costs, in their words:** the day, "Every single day I lose something or forget something. I do try hard." (S16, Mumsnet, 19 Mar 2026); the job, "I sound incompetent some days and can't present in meetings as I just forget things." (S12, Mumsnet, 4 Jun 2025); money, "I have replaced my utility knife more times than I can count as well as my tape measure." (S47, HealthUnlocked, 6 Sep 2025); the people around them, "DH is a big help he puts stuff away for me quite a lot and will do stuff like put my lunch in my work bag in the morning" (S24, Mumsnet, 19 Mar 2026).

## 7. Angles per avatar

Situation language only. None of these says "you have X", names a condition, or promises a result.

- **A1 daily misplacer:** "For the person who does the lap of the house before every trip out, checking pockets, bags and the radiator, because the keys were put somewhere safe." Product: finder tag plus a set place by the door.
- **A2 midlife professional:** "For the person who keeps a wall of sticky notes behind the screen so Monday's meeting goes the way they planned it." Product: desk or wall planner board. (Never mention menopause or "losing words".)
- **A3 overloaded parent:** "For the one who is the whole family's calendar: school forms, the guitar lesson, the dentist, all in one head until bedtime." Product: family planner board and key hooks.
- **A4 knowledge worker:** "For the 2 pm stretch with the inbox open, when a 45-minute block with a timer in view is the only way the report gets written." Product: visual timer, desk lamp.
