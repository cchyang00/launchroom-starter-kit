## Counter-evidence and limits

- **The words are owned by health sellers, but the physical lanes are not.** Supplements and our products do not compete for the same shelf, but they compete for the same reader's attention and for the same Meta wording ("brain fog" is one of the most-bought phrases on the platform). Any ad that leads with the symptom words will bid against 50,000 ads.
- **The strongest open lane for us is the entryway and planner pair with a "never lose it again" angle**, not "brain fog". A wave of copies is arriving in the entryway-organizer lane right now (10 stores in 6 days), so this lane may be crowded by the time a test is ready.
- **A caution on the issue itself:** ISSUE.md rules out memory or focus claims and condition words aimed at the reader, so the only angle left in its lane is the object's job (a hook by the door, a timer on the desk). The advertisers above show that is also how the physical sellers write.
- The all-words queries were sorted by impressions and read to 107 to 188 ads of 50,000 reported (Meta's top figure); the supplement count is a floor. Meta's text search cannot hear speech in video ads.

## Gate result

**I7 · Not owned on Meta: FAIL.** The issue's own words belong to at least 14 supplement and health sellers with ads 30+ days old. Each physical lane is under 3 (visual timer 1, wall planner 1, key finder 1, entryway organizer 0), so a test should be built around the object and its job, never the symptom words, and I7 re-run on that wording first. The full product-level check stays Prove it P1.
