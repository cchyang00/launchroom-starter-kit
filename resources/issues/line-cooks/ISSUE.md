## 1. The issue in one line

Ten to fourteen hours on a kitchen floor, and by the end the knees, feet and ankles are done (the title is one cook's own words, #15). Cooks cycle through $100 to $180 shoes (Crocs, Birkenstock, Dansko, Hoka, Mise), swap socks and shoes mid-shift, and still watch the pair fall apart in 6 to 8 months.

## 2. What it feels like

- **Worn down by the hours:** "i was standing 10+ hours and at the end of the day my feet were killing me" (#15) · "Been using Crocs in the kitchen and my knees are killing me." (#10)
- **Trial and error fatigue:** "Had Converse, had shoes for Crews, had Vans, had danskos which I threw on the garbage within a month. Had Crocs." (#1) · "I spent literally thousands of dollars trying to find shoes that don't hurt my feet and actually work." (#22)
- **Resentment at paying for it twice:** "Not happy about that for an $180 shoe within that period." (#11) · "Kinda regret the extra cash I spend just for the heat." (#12)
- **Pride in the loyal pair:** "Whenever I wear something different I end up aching all over and my knees are very unhappy." (#21)
- **Feeling unseen:** "I think the wrong people in the kitchen got asked. Half of this people are sitting most of their shift in the office." (#1)

### The moment and its cost (I2, checked 2026-10-03)

**The moment that keeps coming back:** hour ten of a double or a Friday service, when the knees and feet give out, on a floor that is wet, greasy and hot. It repeats every shift, and again every time a pair dies.
**What it costs, in their words:** the body ("my knees are killing me", #10; "my feet were killing me", #15), money ("$180 shoe" lasting 6 to 8 months, #11; "thousands of dollars", #22) and the search itself ("Had Converse, had shoes for Crews, had Vans...", #1).
