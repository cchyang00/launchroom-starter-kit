# Issue: "sick of my back and hands hurting after a quilt session"

*One file per issue: `resources/issues/crafters/ISSUE.md`. Method and gate: [workflow/CHECKS.md](../../../workflow/CHECKS.md), Step 1. Researched 2026-10-03 by buyer-researcher (job issues-niche-b-crafters, set B: life stages and hobbies). Every quote has a link and a date; unknown stays unknown.*

**Group:** long-hours needle and machine crafters (knitters, crocheters, quilters, hand-quilters) who feel the hours in their hands, shoulders and back before the project is done. Cross-stitchers are a thin fourth avatar (eyes and light only).

## 1. The issue in one line

"I love this, but by hour two my hands, thumbs, shoulder or back are paying for it": they cut sessions short, change how they hold the needle, wear wrist warmers, and some say they almost gave up the craft.

## 2. What it feels like

- **Fear of losing the hobby**: "Facing the possibility of giving up knitting was a horrible thing for me." (#2) · "I'm guessing I may have to stop this hobby" (#15) · "I wish my right wrist didn't complain so much when I crochet. I miss hooking." (#8)
- **Pacing themselves like an athlete**: "I knit with elbows close to body so my fingers do most of the work, and only for 20 minutes at a time, then haul myself up off the sofa..." (#17) · "I cut in batches so that my hand doesn't get too sore." (#32)
- **Wearing out the body for the love of it**: "...sitting for hours, hunched over a sewing machine and shoving a large quilt around can make my body hurt and my legs start to swell." (#19)
- **Jealous of the pain-free version**: "...except that my hands do not hurt by hour 2." (#38)
- **Quietly worn down by the eyes**: "...yes, my eyes need breaks from it." (#34) · "...my eyes say no" (#37)

## 6. Product opportunities (physical, never eaten) and the gap

**What is crowded (do not lead with these)**, Amazon read-only on 2026-10-03: ergonomic crochet hooks ($9 to $23; one set has 28.2K reviews and "3K+ bought in past month"), thimbles and finger guards ($3.49 to $15.95; "2K+ bought"), ergonomic rotary cutters (Martelli $23 with 3.5K reviews, Olfa $21 with 9.4K), quilting gloves ($6 to $12; one with 1.9K reviews), ergonomic seam rippers ($5 to $14). Every one is cheap and has hundreds of reviews.

**Where the gap is:**
- **Sewing machine tilt platform.** Martelli sells it at $150 to $162 (Amazon, 1 to 2 reviews each; Google also shows $159.99 and a machine-quilting business blog post from Feb 2026). An unbranded "Ergonomic Sewing Machine Tilt Platform, Set of 2" sits at **$31.99 with 24 reviews and no sales badge**. In the forum, one quilter asks about wedges and "the actual tables" (#21), another is about to try door stops (#22), and a user says tilt platforms "make a huge difference" (#20). Price room: $32 listing against $160 brand.
- **Craft lap and arm support pillow.** Listings exist at $22 to $38 ("Crochet Pillow for Lap Support", 292 reviews, "200+ bought"; a 1.1K-review reading pillow with "300+ bought"), but most are titled for gaming or reading. Nobody speaks to the long-hours knitter. One forum mention only (#16): modest evidence, moderate competition.
- **Hook, needle and hand-sewing comfort add-ons** (EVA hook grip sleeves, fingertip pads, wrist yarn guide). The sleeve listings have 0 to 2 reviews at $10 to $16, but the neighbours are the crowded aisle above. Best as a bundle or an add-on, not the lead.
- Not sold by us: heated gloves (battery; the one seen in a forum link is doubted, #7), compression gloves (treatment-sounding), light-up hooks (battery).
