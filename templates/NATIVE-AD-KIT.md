# Native ad kit: fill-in copy for a launch product

Updated November 5, 2026 for our **Day 1–3 Meta Ad Launch Pack (`1–3` Top Viral Creator Videos + `4–5` Direct-Response `4:5` Static Ads)** and **2-Kit Only + `$4.99` Shipping Protection** storefront offer (`>= $45` ideal retail / `>= $24` floor, `<35%` air-shipped landed cost verified on HyperSKU & CJ first). Use it with [the copy brief](COPY-BRIEF.md), [the page formula](PRODUCT-PAGE-COPY.md), and [the Meta Ad Urgency Playbook](../resources/notes/2026-11-04-meta-ad-performance-and-urgency-playbook.md).

**To start writing I need:** product name and exact verified HyperSKU/CJ/Alibaba listing (specs, variants, what is in the box, real video truth frames), verified air-shipped landed cost (`<35%` of retail) and 2-Kit pricing (`1-Kit` + `$7.99` shipping vs. `2-Kit` `SAVE 15%` + Free Tracked Shipping + `$4.99` Shipping Protection), the avatar and situation with 15+ verbatim quotes (`4–5★` Amazon reviews mined FIRST for the 3 best features), and the top 3–5 converting US viral creator `.mp4` videos.

## 1. Day 1–3 Creative Mix: 1–3 Truly Viral Creator Videos + 4–5 Unaware-Scroller DR Static Ads (Owner Rules — November 4–5)

- **Curate Only 1–3 Truly Viral Creator `.mp4` Videos on Day 1–3 (Never Dilute with 15–20 Mediocre Videos):**
  - Owner Rule (Nov 4): *"There was really only 1 viral video for the grimoire and 2 others that were okay. You got a bunch that was low in views and not very viral at all. The most important thing was to get videos that were truly viral, like 1 million views or 500k views. Not low views. We can stick with 1 video or 2 videos only if those are the only ones that are viral."*
  - Launching 15–20 videos starves 80% of them at `$0.00–$0.50` spend on a `$50/day` CBO. Curate only the **top 1–3 highest-converting US viral creator videos** alongside **`4–5` Direct-Response `4:5` static ads**. Once a product survives the End-of-Day 3 Kill Gate with proven sales, build custom **Veo AI video ads** on **Day 4+**.
- **Unaware-Scroller Direct-Response Static Ad Psychology (`4:5`, `1080x1350`):**
  - **NEVER make dry numbered feature-callout diagrams** (e.g., `P007` `ad-c2-blackout-kit-angle` burned `65%` of budget at `1.60%` CTR with `0` page views because people stopped to squint at small boxes without buying).
  - **Every `4:5` DR static ad must follow our 4-Part Direct-Response Formula:**
    1. **Unaware-Scroller Concrete Domino Chain -> Self-Interrogation Question:** Walk a cold scroller through the exact physical sequence of events (*"First the bottled water at the store is gone. Then the food shelves empty. Next the power cuts out. When night falls on your street, what will you do?"*)—never vague insider lines (*"With everything happening in the government..."*).
    2. **3–7 Word Hook Headline (`<0.8s` scan time):** Punchy, high-contrast hook at the top of the image.
    3. **3 Best Buyer-Praised Features Strip:** 3 quick checkmarks or Us-vs-Them callouts sourced from **4–5 star Amazon reviews** so the viewer is pre-sold before clicking.
    4. **1st-Person Avatar Persona POV:** Written in first-person buyer voice (`"I bought this..."`, `Olivia Scott — Verified Buyer`) rather than a corporate store voice.

## 1b. Website vs. Meta Ad Headline Split & Viral Video Hook Harvesting (Owner Hard Rule — November 3–5)

1. **In Meta Ads (`#/create`): Use Urgent, Eye-Catching Reveal & Pain Hooks:**
   - Never use flat product labels (`"Your 2027 Grimoire"`) in Meta ad headlines.
   - Every Meta ad **Headline** MUST be an urgent, curiosity-driving reveal or pain hook combined with a CTA (e.g., ✅ *"You Have to Get This 2027 Journal — Get Yours Now Today! 🌙"*, ✅ *"Keep 2 Full Rooms Lit All Night For $0 — Get Yours Today!"*).
2. **On the Website (`merrivy.com` — `hero.headline` & `purchase.heading`): Use Elevated Boutique / Hardware Titles:**
   - **NEVER** repeat *"You have to get this..."* in `hero.headline` or `purchase.heading` on the website! The Meta ad already did that job.
   - On the website, use an **elevated boutique or hardware title** (`"The Witching Hour 2027 Grimoire Planner"`) while weaving the viral creator's reveal phrasing into **`purchase.hook`** and the **3 creator demo video cards (`demonstrations`)**.
3. **Harvest & Combine Copy Directly from the Top Viral Videos + 4–5★ Amazon Reviews:**
   - Fill out the **Viral Video Hook & Caption Harvest Table** in [COPY-BRIEF.md](COPY-BRIEF.md) before writing a word.
4. **The 3-Part Viral-Hook Ad Copy Formula (Primary Text):**
   - **Line 1 (The Harvested Viral Reveal or Domino-Chain Hook):** Hook the cold scroller before "See more".
   - **Line 2 (The Best-Light Reveal + 3 Buyer-Praised Features):** 1–2 conversational lines in first-person buyer voice + 3 scannable feature bullets from 4–5★ reviews.
   - **Line 3 (2-Kit Offer + Urgency CTA):** Pair with our **2-Kit (`SAVE 15%` + Free Tracked Shipping)** offer and link.

## 2. Five native formats and when each fits

| # | Format | What it looks like | Fits when | Image text | Primary text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Moment photo + caption | A plain, believable photo of the situation with the product in it. It is ours, not a customer's. | The problem is visible in one moment | Hook about the moment | Short, plain note from Merrivy, third person |
| B | Article-style | Headline-led image like an explainer: a clear idea, a few short lines, the product | The product needs a one-minute explanation | Idea + 2-3 short lines | Explain the mechanism, then the offer. Merrivy named; no fake publication |
| C | Note or list | A tidy checklist: "3 things to look for in a [product type]" | The buyer is comparing options or has tried others | The list | Walk the list, say where ours fits and what it does not do |
| D | Problem scene | A wider scene of the friction, product in the corner or arriving in the picture | Cold audience that has not named the problem | The moment, as a line | Describe the situation, then the fix |
| E | Close-up with callouts | The real product in use, 2-3 labelled details (F3) | The feature is the reason to believe | Short feature labels with what each does | Feature, what it does, how it feels |

**Three more formats** (research [B](../resources/research/2026-10-01-native-ads-workflow/B-creatives-copy.md), October 1: the owner's comparison and ease-of-use needs had no format; evidence for F and G is thin, so treat them as tests):

| # | Format | What it looks like | Fits when | Image text | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F | Us vs the usual alternative | Two columns, "the usual [alternative]" against ours, 3-5 rows | The buyer compares, or uses something that falls short | The rows | Name the alternative as a type ("a flat pillow"), never a competitor brand, and never "cheapest" or "best". Every row needs a spec we can show. |
| G | How it works in 3 steps | Three short numbered action steps on the real product photo | Ease of use or "how do I wear or set it up" is the doubt | "1 Put on. 2 Adjust. 3 Done." (example shape) | Action verbs only. Never a line about what happens to the body afterwards. More than three steps: use a carousel. A "how it goes on" label can also be added to a format E callout. |
| H | Offer-first banner | The price or bundle leads | A later, warm round, once the offer is real | The offer in one line | Only a real, configured offer (section 7). A late test, not batch one. |

Also run at least one **Native** ad (little text, a curiosity line) and one **Text-led** ad (callout, steps, list, comparison) in the first batch, because the sources disagree on how much image text wins.

**Ad families.** Meta's own post on its Andromeda retrieval system (December 2, 2024) says nothing about similarity scores; agencies say ads that look alike are treated as one, so we treat that as a working hypothesis, not a rule. Build a **family** from one avatar's evidence: **3-5 concepts**, each a different situation, feature or objection, each drawn in **2-3 formats** from the formats above. A family is 6-15 images, not 15 recolours. Each concept names the group and trigger moment it speaks to and the reason to buy it answers (the issue file's considered-purchase reasons). Source: [issue research method](../resources/research/2026-10-02-issue-method/REPORT.md).

**Voice (owner, October 2, see USER.md).** Write like a confident big brand: short, warm, plain words about what the customer gets ("Secure checkout · 30-day returns · 24/7 support"), not hedged lines ("Real help by email"). The rules in section 4 decide what we may claim; they do not decide how it sounds. When a claim is not allowed, say what the item is and does, confidently, instead of softening the sentence.

Different crops or fonts are not new ads. A new variant changes the situation, the feature or the objection it answers.

## 3. The angle formula

One sentence per angle (from [AVATAR-ANGLE-RESEARCH](../workflow/AVATAR-ANGLE-RESEARCH.md)):

> For [avatar] in [situation], [exact verified feature] helps with [one bounded task], so they can [wanted progress]. Unlike [what they use now], it [supported difference]. Offer: [price, bundle, delivery].

Fill the six parts before writing a word of ad copy:

| Part | Fill in | Source ID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avatar | Who, by what they do (not by age, health or identity) | |
| Situation | The moment and what triggers it | |
| The problem in their words | A short paraphrase of what people say, never merged into one fake quote | |
| What they tried | Workaround or alternative and why it falls short | |
| Why this product fits | The exact feature on the CJ listing, and what it does | |
| Offer | Real price, bundle, shipping, delivery range | |

If "why it fits" has no source, change the angle. Do not make the copy louder.

## 4. Compliance: read before writing, check before showing

**What Meta says.** Meta's Advertising Standards (Personal Attributes policy, page read 2026-10-01 at transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/) say ads must not assert or imply personal attributes, directly or indirectly. The list includes age, disability, physical or mental health, medical conditions, vulnerable financial status, race, religion and sexual orientation. Its disallowed examples include "Do you have diabetes?". Practitioner reports (not Meta text; from the manager's research today) say indirect second-person phrasing about a condition has been enforced since March 2026. We follow the stricter reading. The full text was read through a page-summary tool, so the owner or manager re-reads the live policy before publishing.

**Never write:**
- You/your plus a condition, body part, age or money problem: "your back pain", "Do you have bad knees?", "women over 50", "struggling to pay for...". Describe the **situation** or a **third person**: "For anyone who spends the afternoon on the garden floor."
- A named condition in any person: describe the activity and the item, not the diagnosis.
- Cure, treat, heal, relieve, prevent, "pain-free", "doctor approved". Say what the item physically is and does ("a thick foam pad under the knees"), not what happens to the body.
- A result from wearing or using it (posture, weight, sleep, circulation), a timed result ("in 7 days"), or a body before/after.
- Body-shaming or "fix what is wrong with you" framing.
- Fake reviews, ratings, star counts, "thousands of customers", "best seller", fake doctors or experts, fake news logos or "as seen on", fake countdowns, "only 3 left", or any number we do not hold in a record.
- First-person customer stories we invented ("I was skeptical, but..."). The ad voice is Merrivy. A staged or generated photo is an illustration, not a customer.
- A claim that does not match the exact CJ item: material, size, weight limit, colour, what is in the box. Images must show what ships.
- "Cheapest", "lowest price" or a named competitor comparison, unless we hold proof.

**Do:**
- Keep the seller clear: Merrivy named, ad recognisable as an ad. Native is about fit in the feed, not hiding who is selling.
- Target the audience in Ads Manager settings, not by naming the viewer in the copy.
- Delivery: say only what CJ's processing and transit times back, and use the packet's computed range. The FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR Part 435) needs a reasonable basis for any stated ship time, shipment within 30 days when none is stated, and a notice with a cancel or refund option if we cannot meet it. Re-read the rule text before launch.
- Sale and urgency: only while real (token and end date rules in [PRODUCT-PAGE-COPY](PRODUCT-PAGE-COPY.md#deals-offers-and-copy-tokens)).
- Reviews: leave the proof section out until we have real, permissioned feedback. Marketplace reviews from CJ or AliExpress are not Merrivy reviews.

**Quick swaps**

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "Do you have bad knees?" | "For anyone who spends the afternoon on the ground." |
| "Ends your back pain" | "A [exact feature] for a long day of [task]." |
| "Moms over 40 love this" | "Made for [the activity]." (age goes in targeting, not copy) |
| "Tired of feeling stiff?" | "The end of a long [shift/day]: [what the item does for the task]." |
| "I tried everything until..." | "Here is what is in the box." |
| "Thousands of happy customers" | Nothing, or one real fact we hold in a record |

**Primary text & Headline shape (Viral Video Hook + Best-Light Reveal + Offer — November 3 Hard Rule):**

- **Headline (below video/image):** Must be an **eye-catching reveal hook** harvested or adapted from the top viral creator videos (5–9 words)—**never** a flat product title or generic statement:
  - ❌ Bad (Generic label): *"Your 2027 Grimoire"* / *"The 2027 Dated Grimoire Weekly Planner"* / *"The Viral Holiday Tartan Bow Dress"*
  - ✅ Good (Eye-catching reveal hook): *"You Have to Get This 2027 Planner ✨"* / *"Your Witchy Girlfriend Told Me She Wanted This 🌙"* / *"You Have to See This Holiday Dress On 🎄🎀"*
- **Primary Text (3 conversational beats, blank line between — let the video show the features!):**
  1. **Beat 1 — Viral Creator Opening Hook (above "See more"):** The exact spoken hook or caption from a top-converting viral video that reveals the product in its best light (e.g., *"You have to get this 2027 planner before the new year starts 📖✨"* or *"Nah, this 2027 planner was literally made for your witchy little soul 🌙"*).
  2. **Beat 2 — Best-Light Creator Reveal (1–2 conversational lines, not a dry spec list):** Combine the creator's description of why it's special with the buyer's identity/moment (e.g., *"Every single page feels like opening an ancient book of shadows—moon phases, wheel of the year Sabbats, and weekly spreads you'll actually want to open every morning."*).
  3. **Beat 3 — Our Combined Offer + Action:** Pair their viral hook with our offer and link (e.g., *"✨ Free Tracked Shipping + 4 Free Witchy Sticker Sheets included today → merrivy.com/products/<handle>"*).

## 7. Offer and upsell patterns

The owner prices about $20 above cost and below competitors, and earns extra from add-ons and faster shipping. Every line below holds only if the thing is set up in Shopify and priced from a real margin; the paid price always equals the bag price ([deals rules](PRODUCT-PAGE-COPY.md#deals-offers-and-copy-tokens)). Today's quantity packs charge the full unit price; a real saving needs a configured Shopify discount or bundle, and an add-on needs its own Shopify product (ask the store builder).

| Pattern | Copy shape | Must be true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy 2 | "The pair: [honest reason, e.g. one for each place / one spare]." Add "Save [amount]" only when Shopify shows it. | A configured pair price below 2 x unit. Otherwise reward the pair with free shipping: set the free-shipping threshold so two units clear it, and say "Free shipping over {freeShipping}". |
| Bundle | "[Product] + [item] for [price]." Name every part. | The extra item is a real CJ SKU and is in the box. |
| Cheap add-on | "Add [spare/carry/refill] for [price]." One line, one picture, a tick the shopper chooses. | $5-$12, exact fit for this item, shown on the page or in the bag. Never pre-ticked, never added silently. |
| Faster shipping | "Need it sooner? Choose express at checkout: arrives [range]." | CJ has a faster priced route, the dates come from supplier times, and the Shopify rate exists. The standard line stays visible. |
| Free-shipping bar | "Free shipping over {freeShipping}" | Value comes from `offer.freeShippingOver`. |
| Launch price | "{saleLabel}: {percentOff} off", announcements only | Real compare-at price in Shopify, real `endsAt`. |

## 8. Before the owner sees it

- Claim-source list next to the draft: one row per claim (ID, exact words, source and date, allowed wording, status: supported / needs source / remove).
- Read every line aloud. Any "you/your" next to a body, a condition or an age? Rewrite it.
- Could a competitor paste this onto their product unchanged (LS-037)? Make it specific.
- Ad promise, image and page opening describe the same item and offer; every price, bundle and delivery line matches Shopify.
- Check each placement preview (Feed, Stories, Reels) for crop, text and automatic enhancements. Exact Meta image specs have not been verified here; take them from the live Ads Guide.
- Not published, no budget spent, no supplier or customer contact, until the owner says so.
